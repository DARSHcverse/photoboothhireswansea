Three booths, three quite different events. Most of the confusion comes from the fact that they all "take photos at a party" — but they produce different pictures and suit different rooms.

Here is how to pick.

## The Selfie Pod

**What it is:** a sleek, open-standing pod with a large touchscreen. Guests walk up, tap, and go.

**What the photos look like:** bright, colourful, energetic. Groups pile in. Props get used. It looks like a party.

**Space needed:** the smallest footprint of the three — roughly 2m x 2m including the backdrop.

**Best for:** birthdays, proms, school events, brand activations, and any room where the booth needs to be part of the action rather than tucked away.

**The honest downside:** because it is open, it picks up the room's lighting and noise. In a very dark venue the results are less controlled than an enclosed setup.

## The Glam Booth

**What it is:** the black-and-white, soft-lit, skin-smoothing setup — the one modelled on the celebrity-party look.

**What the photos look like:** genuinely striking. High-contrast monochrome portraits with flattering diffused lighting. These are the images people keep, frame and use as profile pictures.

**Space needed:** around 3m x 2m, and it benefits from a bit of separation from the dance floor.

**Best for:** weddings, engagement parties, milestone birthdays, awards nights — anywhere the photographs are meant to feel elegant rather than silly.

**The honest downside:** it is a slower, more deliberate experience. Guests take their time. If you want rapid-fire volume and chaos, the Selfie Pod does that better.

## The Enclosed Booth

**What it is:** the traditional booth with a curtain. You step inside, the curtain closes, and what happens in there is nobody's business.

**What the photos look like:** uninhibited. The privacy genuinely changes guest behaviour — people who would never pose in the open will do almost anything behind a curtain.

**Space needed:** the largest — around 3m x 2.5m with clearance for the curtain.

**Best for:** weddings with a nostalgic feel, corporate events where staff need permission to loosen up, and any crowd that includes people who would otherwise avoid a booth entirely.

**The honest downside:** it seats fewer people per photo, and the queue moves more slowly because each group is in and out rather than crowding a screen.

## Side by side

| | Selfie Pod | Glam Booth | Enclosed Booth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Look** | Bright, colourful | Black & white, elegant | Classic, candid |
| **Space** | ~2m x 2m | ~3m x 2m | ~3m x 2.5m |
| **Group size** | Large | Medium | Small |
| **Pace** | Fast | Considered | Moderate |
| **Best at** | Proms, birthdays | Weddings, awards | Weddings, corporate |

## So which one?

If you want one rule of thumb:

- **Photographs that look beautiful** → Glam Booth
- **A busy, sociable focal point** → Selfie Pod
- **Getting shy guests involved** → Enclosed Booth

For weddings specifically, most couples land on the Glam Booth, and we think that is the right call — the images age far better than colour party shots.
