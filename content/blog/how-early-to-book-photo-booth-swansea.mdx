Short answer: **if your date is a summer Saturday or anywhere in December, book 9 to 12 months ahead.** For a quiet midweek date in February, a few weeks is usually fine.

That gap between "a year" and "a few weeks" is the whole story, and most advice online gets it wrong by giving one number for both. Here is how it actually works.

## It depends entirely on your date, not your event

Photo booth availability is not really about how popular photo booths are. It is about how many Saturdays exist.

There are 52 Saturdays in a year. Strip out the ones nobody books — deep January, the awkward weekend before Christmas — and the genuinely in-demand ones number perhaps 30. Every wedding, every milestone birthday and every awards night in South Wales is competing for those same dates.

Midweek is a completely different picture. A Tuesday in March has almost no competition, and you could book it a fortnight out without any drama.

## What goes first, in order

**Summer Saturdays, May to September.** The first to go and the ones people are most disappointed to miss. Often booked 12 months or more ahead, because couples book their booth shortly after confirming the venue.

**December, the whole month.** The densest stretch in the calendar, and the one people underestimate most. Corporate Christmas parties run midweek as well as at weekends, so the entire month compresses. December dates routinely go 9 to 12 months out.

**Bank holiday weekends.** Short supply, heavy demand, every year without fail.

**Prom season, late June into July.** Concentrated into roughly three weeks, and schools tend to book as a cluster once one of them confirms.

**Everything else.** January through March, and most midweek dates, stay open far longer. If you are flexible, this is where the availability genuinely is.

## What you actually lose by waiting

Not money — our prices do not rise the closer you get. What you lose is **choice**, and it goes in a predictable order.

**First, your date.** The obvious one. If your venue is booked and your date is fixed, this is the one that hurts.

**Then your booth.** We might have your date free but only one booth type available. If you had your heart set on the Glam Booth for those black-and-white portraits, and only the Selfie Pod is left, that is a real compromise on the photographs you end up with.

**Then your setup.** Specific backdrops, particular strip designs, attendant preferences on the larger packages — the more notice, the more of this is genuinely yours to choose.

**Finally, your planning calm.** Not nothing. Couples who sort the booth early stop thinking about it. Couples who leave it are still making decisions in the month when they have the least capacity for them.

## The honest counterpoint

Last-minute bookings work more often than you would think.

Cancellations happen. Midweek dates sit open. We have set up booths for events confirmed inside a fortnight and they were every bit as good as the ones booked a year out. The booth does not know how much notice it had.

So if you are reading this three weeks before your event, do not assume it is hopeless — just ask. The worst case is we say no, and that takes one message to find out.

What we cannot do is conjure a second booth for a Saturday in July that is already taken. That is the entire risk, and it is why early matters for peak dates specifically rather than as a general rule.

## A rough guide by event

| Event | Sensible booking window |
|---|---|
| Summer wedding (Sat) | 9–12 months |
| Winter wedding | 6–9 months |
| Christmas party | 6–12 months |
| Prom | 6–9 months |
| Corporate / awards | 2–4 months |
| Birthday (midweek) | 3–8 weeks |

These are guides, not rules. The only way to know about *your* date is to ask about your date.

## What booking early actually involves

Less than people expect. A date is held with a deposit, and the balance is not due until before the event — you are not paying for everything a year in advance.

The rate agreed at booking is the rate you pay, so an early booking also fixes your price.

And if your plans shift, talk to us. Date changes are usually workable when there is reasonable notice and the alternative is free.

## The one thing worth doing today

Checking is free and takes a minute. You do not need your final guest count, your timeline or your strip design — just the date and the venue.

If it is available, you will know. If it is not, you will find out now rather than in six months when your options have narrowed.
