The backdrop is the one choice that shows up in every single photo your guests take home. Get it right and the prints look like they belong at your event. Get it wrong and every shot fights the room.

Here is how to pick, and what actually changes the result.

## Start with the room, not the theme

Most people choose a backdrop from a photo on a website. The better starting point is the room it will stand in.

**Look at the wall behind where the booth will go.** A floral backdrop against a heavily patterned function-room carpet and flock wallpaper is a lot of competing detail in one frame. A plain backdrop in that room does more work.

**Look at the lighting.** Dim evening receptions with warm uplighting behave very differently from a bright marquee at 4pm. Dark backdrops absorb light and can make a dim room feel darker still; light backdrops bounce it back.

**Look at what is directly beside it.** Fire exits, radiators, stacked chairs and bar signage all creep into the edges of wide group shots. Tell us what is there and we will position around it.

## The four backdrops we hire

### Black

Sleek and modern. It suits evening receptions and corporate events, and it makes colour pop — bright outfits, props and neon signage all read strongly against it.

**It works best when the room has its own light.** In a dark venue with little lighting, a black backdrop plus low ambient light gives you very moody prints. That is sometimes exactly the look people want, and sometimes a surprise. Worth deciding on purpose.

### White

The most versatile of the four, and the one we suggest when someone genuinely cannot decide. Clean, bright prints, and portrait-style shots that do not date.

It is also the most forgiving: it reflects light back into faces, which flatters guests and keeps prints bright even in a dimmer room. If your event has a strong colour scheme already — flowers, table linen, bridesmaid dresses — white lets that be the colour in the photo rather than competing with it.

### Floral

Soft and playful. A strong fit for weddings, milestone birthdays and bridal events, and reliably the one guests comment on.

**The honest trade-off:** it is the most decorative option, so it carries the most of its own style. If your event has a very specific palette, a floral backdrop is a second pattern in the frame. It is stunning when it complements your flowers, and busy when it argues with them.

### Graduation

Themed for school and university celebrations where the milestone is the point. It signals the occasion in the print itself, so the photo still reads as a graduation years later without anyone needing to explain it.

Best for graduation balls, leavers' events and school celebrations rather than general parties.

## Matching a backdrop to your event

| Event | Usually works best | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wedding, elegant venue | White | Lets your flowers and colours lead |
| Wedding, relaxed or rustic | Floral | Warm and in keeping with the styling |
| Evening reception, dark room | White | Keeps prints bright when the room is not |
| Corporate or awards night | Black | Smart, modern, brand colours stand out |
| 18th, 21st or milestone birthday | Floral or Black | Playful, or sleek, depending on the night |
| Graduation or leavers' ball | Graduation | The occasion is in the photo itself |
| Prom | Black or White | Fast, flattering, suits any colour scheme |

These are starting points rather than rules. If you are torn between two, tell us the venue and we will say which we would use and why.

## Four things worth knowing

**A standard backdrop is included in every package.** It is not an add-on or an upgrade, and it does not change the price. Our [packages](/packages) all include your choice, along with the attendant, unlimited prints and USB images.

**The backdrop does not change the space needed.** We still ask for a clear 3m x 3m area and a standard plug socket within reasonable reach, whichever one you pick.

**You can decide late.** We confirm your strip design before the event, and the backdrop can be settled at the same time. If you are still deciding between two in the week before, that is completely fine.

**It affects the prints more than the room.** A backdrop is only a couple of metres wide. Most guests at your event will barely register it — but every single person who takes a print home will have it in their photo forever. That is the right way to weigh the decision.

## The mistake we see most often

Choosing the backdrop that looks best on its own, rather than the one that looks best *with everything else in the photo*.

Your guests will be wearing your colour scheme. They will be holding props. They may be standing in front of a lit dance floor. The backdrop is one layer of several, and the quietest option usually produces the best photographs — which is why white is our most common recommendation despite being the least exciting to look at on a website.

If you want the backdrop to be the feature, floral is the one that earns it.
